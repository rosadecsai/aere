# Earning the Benefit of the Doubt: An Auditable Authorial-Effort Mechanism for Resolving Peer Review Disagreements

When two referees issue opposed recommendations, editors conventionally recruit a third reviewer—an arbitration heuristic that, in low-consensus fields, resolves disagreement by adding stochastic noise and leaves no verifiable record of the decision. We develop, formally ground, and evaluate the Author Effort Response Evaluation (AERE) mechanism, an auditable alternative in which the deadlocked author faces a public, pre-committed technical-expansion threshold, documents the revision in a ledger of Technical Contribution Units, and the original reviewer dyad verifies that ledger under a conjunctive rule that preserves the skeptic’s veto. We derive the separating equilibrium underlying the contest, specify a complete operational protocol (disciplinary rubric, execution algorithm, calibrated thresholds, worked case study), and evaluate the mechanism in stratified Monte Carlo simulations comprising 133,217 deadlocked manuscripts at the empirically documented low-consensus baseline (κ = 0.30; at least 10⁴ deadlocks per operating point). Relative to third-reviewer arbitration, AERE reduces false rejections from 29.3% to 11.6% (-60.4%) and false acceptances from 15.2% to 4.3% (-71.7%), a 64.2% aggregate error reduction; a five-axis sensitivity suite bounds these gains under auditor degradation, fatigue, and capture. Five adversarial stress tests show attenuation of prestige and demographic bias effects above 91%, filtration of strategic padding reaching 99.2% under strict auditing (versus 87.5% for the third-reviewer model), and a structural fragmentation penalty that collapses salami-sliced revisions. We discuss the implications for research evaluation theory and practice, including the mechanism’s extension to split verdicts in grant-proposal assessment.
